    FastSmoothBuildings improvement plan¶

    What to change in the FastSmoothBuildings library so that it stays within the bands of Buildings on the zone temperatures, the heating power and the heating demand and responds to a parameter change as Buildings does. The plan follows from the study of BESTEST 950FF, 940, 960 and the house before and after renovation and from a reading of the equations of the library (then trano/library/FastModel.mo) against the Buildings components they stand for. Each item names the gap, the evidence, the change, its cost in states or in smoothness (FastSmoothBuildings must stay an explicit, event-free ODE for rumoca and CasADi) and the validation that closes it.

    The study ranks the gaps by what they change. When it started, in Denver, where the air is thin and the BESTEST walls are heavy, FastSmoothBuildings heated 7 to 16 % more than Buildings and responded 12 to 30 % more to the infiltration; in Uccle, with the renovated house, it stayed within 1 % of the meters and 5 % of the sensitivities, except where thermostatic valves share the flow of the circuit (set point response 0.80). Two defects were fixed during the study: the night ventilation that FastSmoothBuildings dropped and the 1 kW leak of its ideal systems. Three stages of changes followed (see Progress); the extended study (sweeps, glazing, sun, gains, sun-space, ventilation, radiators) then found the gaps of the last stage and the ones still open.

    Progress¶

    The first phases are in the library (then trano/library/FastModel.mo), its templates and library.json. Every step kept the models smooth and without events, and every FastSmoothBuildings model still exports to CasADi with rumoca (trano's tests/test_fast_model.py, now tests/test_rumoca.py here). Each step was measured on the BESTEST cases FastSmoothBuildings can describe (23 of 27 until the four with shading could run, all 27 since) against the band of the standard and against the values of Buildings for the same YAML:

    What each step did:

    • Air density at the site (phase 1). The weather bus carries the atmospheric pressure (101325 Pa, or the pressure of the site's altitude when the YAML reads it from the weather file), and the zone uses the ideal-gas density at that pressure and its air temperature. The heating of the Denver cases fell from +9 % to −0.4 % of Buildings. It also showed that the excess infiltration had hidden a heat surplus: with it gone, FastSmoothBuildings cooled 5 % more than Buildings and its free-floating peaks rose by up to 4 K above those of Buildings.
    • Smooth limits that reach their bounds (phase 1). smoothLimit is exactly its bound beyond a quadratic blend and never leaves the range; it used to saturate 0.1 % inside, which made the ideal systems leak 1 kW of heating and of cooling at all times. The solar angles keep the interior limit (smoothLimitInside), acos being singular at the bounds.
    • Discretised walls (phase 2). The closed-loop model splits each layer into the states Buildings gives it, at most three sublayers; FastSmoothBuildingsMPC keeps one node per layer boundary, its states being the decision variables of the optimal control. The reheat peak of 940 after the night set-back fell from +8 % to +2 % of Buildings. The single glazing of 670 runs at last: its record wrote an empty array, {}, that OpenModelica does not parse.
    • Long-wave exchange in the room around the mean surface temperature when the YAML sets linearize_emissive_power: false, as every BESTEST case does (Buildings then exchanges exactly). The cooling excess fell by about half a point and the free-floating peaks by up to 0.8 K.
    • Exact long-wave exchange with sky and ground for outer walls and windows, \(\sigma\varepsilon A (T^4 - T_{env}^4)\) instead of its linearisation at \(T_{env}\): a sun-lit outer surface loses a quarter more at 60 degC. The cooling excess fell from +5.0 to +2.4 % on average.
    • Gas gaps at the temperatures of their panes (phase 3). FastSmoothBuildings held each gap of a glazing at its EN 673 conductance, computed by trano's Python at 10 degC; Buildings recomputes the long-wave exchange across the gap (\(\sigma (T_a^4 - T_b^4)/(1/\varepsilon_a + 1/\varepsilon_b - 1)\), which grows as \(T^3\)) and the convection of the gas (Wright's correlations of the vertical cavity) at the temperatures of the panes. A sun-lit glazing at 40 degC conducts a fifth more than at 10 degC, so FastSmoothBuildings kept too much of the sun in the zones that nothing cools. Envelope.Functions.gapConductance now computes both in Modelica from the data of the YAML (the conductivity of the panes, the properties of the gas, the emissivities facing each gap), an explicit and smooth function of the pane temperatures; GGap stays a parameter (the conductance at the EN 673 state, scaled by the temperatures), so that mpctrano still calibrates it. The free-floating temperatures came within 0.38 K of Buildings on average (0.80 K before) and 1.70 K at the hottest hour (3.52 K); 680FF went from 1.17 to 0.53 K hourly RMSE.
    • Small hydraulic links (phase 3). The study blamed the flow law for the set point responses of the renovated house; trano's Buildings valves and radiators are linearised too (linearized=true), with the same conductances as FastSmoothBuildings. Measured branch by branch, the gap was elsewhere: with the living room valve fully open, FastSmoothBuildings passed 36.6 g/s against 43.9 g/s in Buildings, because the components that have no resistance in Buildings (the sensor feeding the six radiators, the water side of the heat pump) had a linear one of 1000 Pa at their nominal flow, a fifth of the head of a radiator branch. At 100 Pa, the living room warms by 0.75 K with a set point +1 K (Buildings 0.80 K, before 0.71 K) and the kitchen by 0.64 K (0.71, 0.60).
    • Layers split in Modelica (phase 3). The construction record carries the layers of the YAML and the number of sublayers of each (the states Buildings gives it, at most 3; one for FastSmoothBuildingsMPC); PartialOpaque splits them. The counts stay literal in the record: rumoca 0.10.3 sizes arrays only from literal parameters (not from a sum, nor from a default of the record). Same results within 3e-5. The walls name T the temperatures of the layer boundaries, the states of both FastSmoothBuildings and FastSmoothBuildingsMPC, and TSub the nodes inside the layers (FastSmoothBuildings only): a state of one model copied into the other by its name stays at its place. Before, T[2] of the MPC model was a layer boundary and T[2] of the closed-loop model a node inside the first layer; mpctrano's spin-up, which copies the states by name, started the MPC model with the inner surfaces of its walls inside the insulation.
    • The house before renovation (study, fourth run, no change of the library). The gas boiler at 75 degC, its three-way valve and six radiators with thermostatic valves follow Buildings in closed loop: rooms within 0.02 to 0.09 K hour by hour, heating +1.7 %, gas +1.9 %, and the six changes that move the results (cavity insulation, airtightness, supply temperature, radiator size, set point) alike, gas included. It exports to CasADi (160 states as FastSmoothBuildingsMPC, 224 in closed loop) and IPOPT solves its optimal control with the boiler and the valves as controls. The study now reads the gas and the electricity of both libraries from the energy meters: samples of the powers five minutes apart put the gas 0.6 to 0.75 % off when the boiler fires at full power for two minutes as the valves open; per unit of heat FastSmoothBuildings burns the same gas as Buildings within 0.2 %.
    • Overhangs and side fins (phase 4). The window takes the geometry of the YAML (overhang, side_fins) and shades its direct irradiance by the sunlit fraction of Buildings.HeatTransfer.Windows.Overhang and SideFins, ported to Envelope.Functions.overhangSunlitFraction and sideFinsSunlitFraction: the same areas of the shadow in the plane of the window, with smoothMax/smoothMin in place of max/min, the tangents written as a sine over a cosine kept away from zero, and regStep blends where Buildings switches (the sun behind the wall, below the horizon). With both, the fractions combine as in Buildings (\(f_{ove} + f_{fin} - 1\), kept above 0.001). The diffuse irradiance is not shaded, as in Buildings. A unit test checks the fractions against their closed form at a few sun positions. The four shading cases run and pass every KPI, within 2.2 % of Buildings (910 peak cooling), and FastSmoothBuildings gates in BESTEST as Buildings and IDEAS do. A window without shading keeps its fraction at 1 through an evaluated parameter: the six-zone house still exports with rumoca (189 states, 28 inputs for FastSmoothBuildingsMPC) and IPOPT solves its 12-hour optimal control in about a minute.

    Still open. The free-floating zones of the light, well-insulated constructions still peak 1 to 1.7 K above Buildings (600FF, 650FF, 680FF) and the 960 sun-space is 0.4 K warm on average. FastSmoothBuildings responds 8 % less than Buildings to the solar absorptance of the outer surfaces (priority 8). In the renovated house, the supply water is 0.4 K cooler when the heat pump runs at full load, and the responses to the set point (heating 0.87, electricity 0.85), to the buffer and to the peaks go through the heat pump (priority 9). Unbalanced ventilation, wind- and stack-driven infiltration and the ISO 13370 ground are not modelled (priority 6). The proposals give a formulation of each that keeps the model explicit and smooth.

    Gaps found by reading the equations¶

    These differences exist whatever the case; the study measures how much each of them weighs.

    1. One node per layer boundary in the opaque constructions¶

    Envelope.PartialOpaque puts one capacity at each boundary between the layers of a construction (\(n\) layers, \(n + 1\) nodes), half of each adjacent layer lumped into the node. Buildings discretises each layer into nSta states; the BESTEST cases ask for 18 states per 0.2 m of layer (number_of_states in the YAML, ignored by FastSmoothBuildings). A 0.1 m concrete block is one lumped capacity in FastSmoothBuildings and about nine in Buildings. The conductance of a layer and its total capacity are the same, so the steady-state heat loss is identical and a conductivity change moves the demand alike; what differs is the dynamics: the lumped wall responds faster to a step of the room or outdoor temperature and stores the diurnal solar gain differently. That weighs on the free-floating extremes, on the morning reheat peak after a set-back and on the response to a change of the thermal mass.

    Change: discretise each layer into nSta nodes as Buildings does, with nSta read from number_of_states (default 1 to keep today's models) and a cap for the MPC export; or, cheaper, two nodes per layer thicker than a threshold. Cost: states (a heavy BESTEST wall goes from 4 to about 12 nodes), none in smoothness.

    2. Air density and the site pressure¶

    The zone uses \(\rho = 1.2\) kg/m3 for the infiltration (rhoAir*V*ACH/3600). Trano's Buildings zone takes the density of the air at the atmospheric pressure of the weather file when the YAML asks for it (atmospheric_pressure_source: File, as the BESTEST cases do): in Denver, 1609 m above sea level, that is about 0.98 kg/m3. The infiltration heat flow of FastSmoothBuildings is then about 20 % larger than that of Buildings for the same air change rate, and so is its response to a change of the air change rate. The scheduled night ventilation is a mass flow in both libraries and is not affected.

    Change: rhoAir from the pressure of the weather bus (pAtm, ideal gas at the zone temperature or at 20 degC), one algebraic expression. Cost: none.

    3. Long-wave exchange linearised at 20 degC¶

    The radiant star of the zone uses \(G_i = 4\sigma T_0^3 \varepsilon_i A_i\) at \(T_0 = 20\) degC; the BESTEST cases ask Buildings for the non-linear exchange (linearize_emissive_power: false). The error grows with the spread of the surface temperatures: a sun-lit floor at 35 degC and a window at 5 degC in winter, the free-floating zone at 60 degC in summer. Change: linearise around the current mean surface temperature (\(T_0 = T_{rad}\) of the previous step is not available in an ODE; \(T_0^3\) as a smooth function of the mean surface temperature is), or keep the exact \(\sigma \varepsilon (T_i^4 - T_{rad}^4)\) with the star temperature solved implicitly. Cost: the exact form adds an algebraic loop; the moving linearisation adds none.

    4. Controllers without events and without safety times¶

    A hysteresis switches over tau (10 s by default, a parameter since this branch) instead of at an instant, and the heat pump has no minimum on and off times. The heat pump starts and stops at other instants than in Buildings, which shows in the water temperatures and the hour-by-hour heat of the radiators (see the replay validation), less in the zones and the meters. Change: a smooth minimum on/off time (a first-order timer gating the compressor), and smooth_controllers widths chosen per use (simulation: narrow; calibration: the x5, 600 s of mpctrano). Cost: one state per heat pump.

    5. Hydraulics linearised, one production per circuit, mixing valves only¶

    Pressure-driven flows with resistances linearised at their nominal flows, as trano's Buildings models are (linearized=true on the valves and radiators: the same conductances), and a small resistance for the components that have none in Buildings, each inlet a pressure node of small compliance so that the flows stay explicit. That small resistance was 1000 Pa at the nominal flow, a fifth of the head of a radiator branch behind a six-way manifold; it is 100 Pa since phase 3 (2 % of the branch). A pressure node keeps its last value when its pump stops (the head of a stopped pump reads 20 kPa instead of 0), which changes nothing but the reported head.

    6. Heat pump performance map¶

    The Vitocal 251-A08 table of Buildings is scaled to the capacity of the YAML, interpolated and smoothed in the source temperature, quadratic in the condenser temperature; the condenser outlet is approximated from the buffer temperature. Change: fit the condenser side on the EN 14511 table too (bilinear smooth interpolation), keep the scaling. Cost: none in states.

    7. Balanced ventilation and fans at constant pressure¶

    The unit is balanced (supply = extract) while in Buildings part of the supply leaves by the infiltration paths, so that the recovery works on a smaller extract stream; the fans run at constant pressure rise. Change: an extract flow equal to the supply minus the infiltration pushed out, as the pressures of Buildings set it. Cost: none.

    8. Missing features¶

    Storage tanks on boilers, water-to-water heat pumps, chillers, domestic hot water, batteries, EV chargers, VAV, air handling units other than system D, power sensors, moisture, wind- and stack-driven infiltration, the ISO 13370 ground of IDEAS. The night ventilation schedule and the overhangs and side fins (BESTEST 610, 630, 910, 930) were missing and are added by this branch.

    Proposals for the remaining gaps¶

    What rumoca and CasADi need of FastSmoothBuildings, learnt on the way: an explicit ODE (every algebraic variable a function of the states, the inputs and the parameters: no algebraic loop, a pressure node of small compliance where Buildings solves the flows of a circuit), once continuously differentiable equations without events (regStep, smoothMax, \(\sqrt{x^2 + \delta^2}\) for \(|x|\), no when, pre or sample), and, for rumoca 0.10.3, array sizes and subscripts given by literal parameters: not a sum, not the result of a function, not a default of a record (the sublayers of a construction are written out by trano for that reason), and no loop in a function over a parameter that stays tunable. Each proposal below keeps to that; its cost is in states and in parameters, its gate in the study or in BESTEST.

    Gap Proposal Cost Gate
    Response to the outer solar absorptance (0.91) Find where the heat goes: compare the outer surface temperatures and the conduction into the room of a sun-lit wall in both libraries (the irradiance, the outside convection with its wind modifier, the sky exchange and the sublayers, up to 9, are ruled out); the room-side long-wave exchange of the opaque surfaces (radiant star against Buildings' radiosity) is the next suspect none until the cause is known absorptance ratios 0.95 to 1.05
    Supply water at full load The condenser outlet and the capacity from the EN 14511 table of the heat pump, interpolated as the other optics and curves of FastSmoothBuildings are (a polynomial fit evaluated in Modelica, smooth in the source and condenser temperatures) instead of the buffer temperature parameters only supply within 0.2 K of Buildings at full load, set point responses within 5 %
    Heat pump safety times A first-order lag on the compressor command, \(\tau \dot y = y_{on} - y\), with \(\tau\) a third of the minimum run time of the YAML: the compressor follows its hysteresis but cannot start and stop faster than the safety times allow, on average, without an event (Buildings holds each start and stop exactly, with events) one state per heat pump buffer and supply peak ratios 0.8 to 1.2

    Plan¶

    Phase 1: the steady state (days)¶

    1. Air density at the site. PartialZone takes the atmospheric pressure of the weather bus (pAtm, already in the weather data of trano) and computes \(\rho = p_{atm} / (R_{air} T_{air})\) for the infiltration and the scheduled ventilation of the zone, as Buildings.ThermalZones.Detailed.MixedAir does through its medium (an ideal gas at the pressure of the site); the heat capacity of the air volume takes the same density. For the MPC export the pressure is a disturbance already, so the ODE stays explicit. Gate: 940 heating within 5 % of Buildings (today +15.6 %), 940 infiltration ratio 0.9 to 1.1 (today 1.30), the renovated house unchanged within 0.5 %.
    2. Smooth limits that reach their bounds. Replace smoothLimit(x, l, u, dX) (saturating at \(l + dX\) and \(u - dX\)) by a limit whose smooth transitions lie inside \([l, u]\) and end exactly on the bounds (the regStep blends of smoothMax and smoothMin shifted inwards by \(dX\), clipped so that they cannot overshoot), in every controller and actuator, and drop the per-component rescaling added during the study. Gate: a unit test on the controllers (saturated output exactly 0 and 1, no output outside \([0, 1]\)), the ideal-system test of the rumoca tests, BESTEST cooling and heating of FastSmoothBuildings within their bands.

    Phase 2: the dynamics (weeks)¶

    1. Layers discretised as Buildings does. PartialOpaque gets nSta[nLay] (from number_of_states of the YAML, which trano already renders for Buildings, scaled to the layer thickness as nStaRef does), each layer split into nSta conductances and capacities. The default keeps today's model (one node per boundary) for MPC, where the state count matters; the closed-loop FastSmoothBuildings takes the YAML. A middle option is a minimum of two nodes for a layer whose time constant exceeds a few hours. Gate: 940 peak heating within 5 % (today +12 %), night heating within 20 kWh, 950FF mass ratios 0.9 to 1.1 (today 1.13 to 1.45), sun-space bias within 0.2 K (today +0.53 K), the MPC export of the renovated house below 1.5 times its states.
    2. Long-wave exchange around the current temperatures. Linearise each radiant conductance at the mean of the surface temperatures of the zone (\(G_i = 4\sigma\varepsilon_i A_i \bar T^3\), \(\bar T\) a smooth function of the states) instead of 20 degC, or keep the exact \(\sigma\varepsilon(T_i^4 - T_{rad}^4)\) with the star solved implicitly in the closed-loop model only. Gate: sun-space RMSE below 0.5 K (today 0.87 K), 960 and 600FF/900FF extremes within 0.5 K of Buildings.

    Phase 3: the systems (weeks)¶

    1. Flow sharing between radiator branches. Done differently: the flow laws are the same as trano's Buildings models (linearised); the resistances of the links that Buildings does not have are ten times smaller (phase 3). Gate kept: the set point and radiator responses of the renovated house within 10 %.
    2. Heat pump safety times and condenser. Minimum on and off times as a smooth timer state gating the compressor, and the condenser outlet from the EN 14511 table rather than the buffer temperature. Gate: the water temperatures of the replay validation within 1 K (today about 2 K in closed loop), buffer and supply peak ratios 0.8 to 1.2.

    Phase 4: coverage¶

    1. Overhangs and side fins (Buildings.HeatTransfer.Windows.Overhang and SideFins geometry, a smooth shaded fraction of the direct irradiance), so that BESTEST 610, 630, 910 and 930 run with FastSmoothBuildings. Done: the four cases inside their bands, within 2.2 % of Buildings.
    2. Unbalanced ventilation (extract = supply minus the air pushed out by the infiltration paths), wind- and stack-driven infiltration, the ISO 13370 ground of IDEAS for the comparisons with IDEAS.

    Gates kept after the plan¶

    • The study runs on every change of the library (then trano/library/FastModel.mo) or of the FastSmoothBuildings templates (the workflow does so when the commit carries [study]); a test reads its committed results and fails when a zone RMSE exceeds 0.5 K (1 K for a free-floating BESTEST zone), a heating demand deviates by more than 10 %, or a sensitivity ratio leaves 0.8 to 1.25, with the deviations understood listed as BESTEST does.
    • FastSmoothBuildings gates in the BESTEST validation as Buildings and IDEAS do (pytest -m bestest, the workflow bestest.yaml): its 27 cases inside their bands, the direction of every change between two cases as the reference programs, and frozen values (expected/FastSmoothBuildings.json) that catch the drifts inside the bands.
